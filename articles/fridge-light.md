## Abstract

The question of whether the light inside a refrigerator remains illuminated after the door has been closed has occupied the domestic imagination for generations.

Despite the apparent simplicity of the question, direct observation is complicated by the fact that opening the refrigerator door necessarily causes the light to illuminate.

This paper presents a preliminary investigation into the problem using repeated observations, mechanical inference, and one extremely long piece of string.

The results suggest that the refrigerator light is probably not illuminated when the door is closed.

However, the possibility cannot be established with absolute certainty.

## Introduction

Few household appliances possess such a strange relationship with observation as the refrigerator.

When the door is open, the interior is visibly illuminated.

When the door is closed, the interior cannot be observed.

The natural question is therefore:

**What happens to the light?**

Most modern refrigerators contain a mechanism designed to switch the light off when the door closes. This would appear to resolve the question.

It does not.

The possibility remains that the mechanism fails.

Or that it switches the light off only most of the time.

Or that the light remains on and simply becomes very bored.

The present study investigates these possibilities.

## Methodology

A standard domestic refrigerator was selected for observation.

Three experimental approaches were attempted.

### Experiment One: Rapid Closure

The refrigerator door was opened and the light observed.

The door was then closed rapidly.

No light was visible.

The door was immediately reopened.

The light was visible.

This result was inconclusive.

### Experiment Two: Observation Through the Door Seal

The researcher attempted to observe the interior through the gap between the door and the refrigerator body.

No useful observations were obtained.

The researcher did, however, become aware that the refrigerator contained significantly less food than anticipated.

### Experiment Three: External Witness

A second researcher was instructed to close the refrigerator door while the first researcher remained nearby.

The second researcher reported that the light turned off.

When asked how they knew, they stated:

> "Because that's what it's supposed to do."

This was deemed methodologically unsatisfactory.

## Theoretical Considerations

The central difficulty of the refrigerator-light problem is observational.

The observer requires the door to be open in order to observe the light.

Opening the door changes the state being observed.

This produces a peculiar domestic version of the observer effect.

The refrigerator therefore possesses two states:

**Observed state:** light on.

**Unobserved state:** probably light off.

The distinction between these states cannot be completely eliminated through conventional observation.

## Discussion

The evidence strongly supports the hypothesis that refrigerator lights switch off when the door is closed.

Nevertheless, the authors acknowledge that the experiment does not provide direct evidence.

There remains a small but philosophically significant possibility that refrigerator lights remain illuminated continuously and that millions of households are collectively wasting electricity without realising it.

Further investigation would require a camera to be placed inside the refrigerator.

This approach was considered.

The camera would then need to be connected to a power source.

The power cable would prevent the door from closing.

The problem therefore remains unresolved.

## Conclusion

The present study finds no compelling evidence that refrigerator lights remain on when the door is closed.

The conventional explanation — that the light switches off — is considered highly probable.

However, the question cannot be regarded as absolutely settled.

The authors recommend that future refrigerator manufacturers provide a small external indicator showing the current status of the interior light.

They further recommend that this indicator be completely unnecessary.
