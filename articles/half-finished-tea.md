## Abstract

This paper examines the ontological status of a half-finished cup of tea.

At what point does tea cease to be an active beverage and become merely a cup containing evidence that a beverage once existed?

Drawing upon observations from domestic and workplace environments, this paper proposes several possible classifications.

## The Problem

A freshly made cup of tea is unambiguously a beverage.

An empty cup is unambiguously not a beverage.

The half-finished cup occupies a considerably more complicated position.

It may be:

- A beverage awaiting completion.
- A beverage that has been abandoned.
- A future beverage.
- A historical beverage.
- A cup of tea that one continues to believe one will return to.

## Discussion

The temporal dimension appears particularly significant.

A cup left unattended for three minutes is generally considered recoverable.

A cup left unattended for three hours may be regarded with suspicion.

A cup discovered the following morning presents an entirely different category of problem.

## Conclusion

Further research is required.

The authors recommend making a fresh cup.
